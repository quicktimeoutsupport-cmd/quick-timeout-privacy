EAT.RUN. / iPhone · iPad

From today’s food to an estimated running distance

Before heading out, try looking at today’s food in kilometres. EAT.RUN. first accounts for your estimated daily energy needs, then shows a running-distance estimate only for calories above that full-day baseline.

Download EAT.RUN. · App Store

iPhone · iPad · Free · Contains banner ads

What goes into the estimate?

Height, weight, age, sex and usual activity level help estimate your full-day energy needs. The app totals the calories in today’s food and portions, then checks whether they exceed that baseline.

  1. Estimate full-day energy needs from your profile.
  2. Add the calories in today’s food entries.
  3. Subtract daily energy needs and use only a positive surplus.
  4. Display a distance using the rough approximation of 1 kcal per kilogram of body weight per kilometre.

Try it before your run

Set up your profile, then choose Portion → Food Library on Home, find a food and select its amount. Choosing the portion saves the entry. After entering today’s food, check the KM number on Home. You can enter meals as you eat or add them together before heading out.

What does 0 km mean?

The food entered so far is at or below your estimated full-day energy needs. A partly entered day can also show 0 km; adding more food may change the number. It does not mean you should skip exercise or eat more.

A playful reference for thinking about your day

Calories and distance are estimates, not a safe running target, training prescription or weight-loss promise. Food does not need to be paid back through exercise. The app does not measure running time, pace, GPS routes or completed workouts.
